Counts are measurements, not sessions: a cell measured three times on the same card reads 3, and a configuration repeated five times contributes 5. FP16 is dense because every quantized run also measures its own FP16 baseline on the same card, and each baseline counts in its own right: 72 quantized measurements plus 72 baselines. The “42 rows” quoted elsewhere on this site counts distinct configurations, not repeats — same data, different unit. A quantized number with no same-session baseline cannot produce a ΔE% and is not counted at all. Contributors so far: @gkgoing, @hongping-zh.

Why ≥ 3 and not 1? One measurement is an anecdote about a card on a particular afternoon. Our own RTX 4090 INT8 cell moved by 12.5 percentage points across three replicates of the same configuration, which is exactly why a single point does not close a cell.

The fastest cell to fill

Any of the four batch-4 cells. They need a card you already have and one parameter change, and they are the boundary between "we measured batch 1" and "we know what batch size does" — which is also the gap between this dataset and the large-batch datacentre leaderboards it cannot currently be compared with.
